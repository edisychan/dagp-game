How is a fit measured?

Write the equation as f(x, y) = 0. Evaluate f at every dot, square the results, then take their average. This is the mean squared error shown in the game. The orange line shows where the tested equation equals zero. It can have extra branches away from the dots because only the values at the dots enter the error.

What does the purple band show? Changing the limit widens or narrows the region where f(x, y)² ≤ ε² for the displayed equation. That test applies at each location. To decide whether the equation itself passes, we compare its average squared value across all 300 dots with the limit. Some dots can fall outside the band even when the equation passes.

Why 0.0005? For the simulated circles in the study, the expected degree‑2 error was about 0.00026. The authors chose a limit roughly twice that size. In Explore, the same dots let you see what a stricter or looser limit admits.

How does this give the dAGP count? We fix the coefficient length at one in an orthonormal Legendre basis. Otherwise, shrinking all the coefficients could make any error tiny. At each maximum degree m, the eigenvalues of the empirical moment matrix give the errors of independent coefficient directions. Counting those at or below the chosen limit ε² gives one entry of the dAGP signature.

These are the poster’s three simulated samples, with 300 points per loop and coordinate noise of standard deviation 0.01. The spectra and lowest-error curves were computed from those points using the singular value decomposition of the feature matrix divided by √300. The game compares the stored errors with your chosen limit.
